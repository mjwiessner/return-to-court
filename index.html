Clinical note, not a restriction: your surgeon/PT cleared you for everything, including jumping and cutting — that stands. But this is a second ACL on the same knee, the second graft is an allograft, and there's a family history of knee laxity. Full clearance means the tissue can tolerate load; it doesn't mean the movement pattern is already bulletproof. So Weeks 1–2 still spend real time on landing mechanics before loading jumps hard — not because you're held back, but because that's how you keep a twice-rebuilt knee out of a third surgery. If you ever get swelling, instability, or sharp pain, stop that movement and tell your surgeon/PT before pushing through it.
Shoulder: pain at the top/back of the right shoulder blade during overhead work (serving, hitting) usually points to a scapular control or rotator-cuff endurance issue rather than a structural tear — but I haven't examined you, so that's a working theory, not a diagnosis. Pulling volume runs roughly double pressing volume all 12 weeks, and cuff/scap work is on every upper day. If it hasn't improved in 4–6 weeks, get it looked at.

Before every session

5–8 min: easy bike or incline walk, leg swings (front/side) ×10 each, hip airplane ×5/side, band external rotations ×15, 2–3 easy warm-up sets of the day's first lift before working weight.
